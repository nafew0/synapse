# Response to reviewers

A point-by-point reply that lets the editor check every change in under a minute.

## Layout

Opening letter (3–5 lines): thank the editor and reviewers, summarise the main changes, say how
changes are marked (tracked changes, highlighted text, or blue font).

Then, per reviewer, a table (or numbered blocks for chat):

| # | Reviewer comment (quoted in full) | Response | Change and location |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1.1 | "The sample size is not justified." | We agree. We have added the power analysis used to determine the sample size. | Section 3.3, p. 7, lines 152–158 |
| R1.2 | "Consider a longitudinal design." | We appreciate the suggestion. A longitudinal design was not feasible within the funding period; we now state this as a limitation and a direction for future work. | Section 5.4, p. 14, lines 301–305 |

Rules:

- Quote every comment verbatim, including ones you disagree with; split compound comments into
  parts (R1.3a, R1.3b).
- Answer each one: agree and change, partly agree, or disagree with reasons and evidence.
- Give the exact change: new text quoted or summarised, and its section, page and line in the
  revised manuscript. If the user has not given page or line numbers, use `[p. X, lines Y–Z]`
  placeholders and list them in the reply.
- Never claim a change was made that the user has not made or asked you to make. When drafting the
  response before the revision, mark changes as "to be made" or ask.
- New references added in response must come from the user or a search in this conversation.

## Tone phrases

- "We thank the reviewer for this helpful comment."
- "We agree, and have revised … accordingly."
- "We have clarified this in …"
- "We respectfully disagree, because … ; we have, however, added … to make our reasoning clearer."
- "This is beyond the scope of the present study; we have noted it as a limitation (Section …)."

Avoid "the reviewer misunderstood"; say "we have clarified the text to avoid this reading".

## Common editor complaints

Comments skipped or merged; responses without locations; changes claimed but not in the manuscript;
defensive tone; a response much longer than the change it describes.
