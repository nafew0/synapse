# Journal reviewer report

When the journal supplies a guideline or comment sheet, follow its headings, rating scales and
recommendation categories exactly; fill the sheet itself when one is attached. Typical report:
500–1,500 words. Comments to the editor (confidential) stay separate from comments to the authors.

## Structure

1. **Summary of the manuscript** (1 paragraph): question, design, data, main findings and claimed
   contribution, in your own words. Shows the authors you understood it.
2. **Overall assessment** (1 paragraph): strengths first, then the main concerns, and whether the
   conclusions follow from the evidence.
3. **Major comments** (numbered): issues that affect validity or the conclusions — the gap and
   contribution, design, sampling, measurement, analysis, alternative explanations, overclaiming,
   missing key literature. Each: the problem, where (section, page, line), why it matters, what
   would fix it.
4. **Minor comments** (numbered, by location): clarity, terminology, figures and tables, reference
   formatting, language.
5. **Recommendation**: the journal's categories, usually accept / minor revision / major revision /
   reject (and resubmit). Give the reason in one or two sentences, in the confidential comments if
   the journal asks for that.

## Checklist for the reviewer

Originality and relevance to the journal's scope; clear gap; appropriate methods; adequate sample;
valid and reliable measures; correct statistics; results support conclusions; limitations stated;
ethics approval; references current and accurate; language quality.

## Integrity

- Base every comment on the manuscript the user supplied. Quote line or section numbers.
- When suggesting missing literature, name only works the user supplied or that a search in this
  conversation found; otherwise describe the kind of work ("recent studies on X in South Asia").
- Do not judge novelty from memory; say "the authors should position this against recent work on X".
- Tone: specific, respectful, constructive; criticise the work, not the authors.
