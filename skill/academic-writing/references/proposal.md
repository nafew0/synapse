# Research proposal

Use the funder's or university's form when supplied; map these sections onto its headings. Typical
length 2,000–5,000 words (PhD proposal 5,000–10,000).

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Title | Specific: variables, population, context. |
| Summary | 200–300 words: problem, aim, method, expected outcome. |
| Background and rationale | The problem with evidence; why now; why here. |
| Literature review and gap | Brief synthesis by theme ending in the gap (see `literature-review.md`). |
| Aim, objectives, questions or hypotheses | One aim; 3–5 SMART objectives, each matched to a question. |
| Methodology | Design, population and sampling, sample size basis, instruments, data collection, analysis per objective, ethics. |
| Expected outcomes and significance | Outputs (papers, datasets, policy brief, prototype) and who uses them. |
| Work plan and timeline | Gantt-style table: activities × months or quarters, milestones. |
| Budget | Table by category: personnel, equipment, consumables, travel, data collection, dissemination, contingency; unit cost × quantity; currency stated (LKR, BDT). Amounts only from the user or as `[amount]`. Short justification per line. |
| Risks and mitigation | 3–5 risks with mitigation. |
| Team | Roles and relevant expertise (the user's details only). |
| References | Only works cited. |

## Common reviewer complaints

- Objectives not measurable or not matched to methods; scope too large for the time and money.
- Budget items not justified or not linked to activities; totals do not add up.
- Ethics, data management and risks missing.
